Forest Landscape Planning

Forest landscape plans (FLP) establish clear outcomes for the management of forest resource values within defined areas. British Columbia’s new forest landscape plans, and the subsequent forest operations plans that will be developed by license holders, will replace forest stewardship plans as part of changes to British Columbia’s forest management regime under the Forest and Range Practices Act (FRPA).

Forest Landscape Plans (FLPs) utilize high-level strategic land use planning direction to a specific forest management area and bridge the gap between strategic land use planning and operational/site-level planning. FLPs will result in deeper engagement and greater transparency with local governments and interest groups to guide forest management direction. FLPs are developed in partnership with First Nations and in collaboration with forest licensees, and through structured engagement with local communities, interest groups, and the public at key milestones of the planning process. Once complete, forest landscape plans will be established by the Chief Forester, in consultation and cooperation with First Nations communities.

Public engagement is focused on informing audiences about the status of FLP projects, as well as opportunities to review and comment on key project deliverables as they are developed.

Steps for Completing a Forest Landscape Plan

Pre-Planning

Pre-planning for a Forest Landscape Plan (FLP) includes exploring First Nation and provincial government interests in an FLP. Key activities include delineating a planning area, scoping community and other interested parties’ interests in a plan, and describing the scope, structure, and vision of the FLP project. Deliverables or major milestones include a Terms of Reference document, work plan, and defined First Nation consultation and cooperation process(es).

Values identification and Assessment

Values identification and assessment for FLP includes establishing planning tables, a structure for coordinating activities and technical working groups. Key activities include documenting current forest management practices for planning values (involves collating information to identify current condition of the forest), identifying information gaps, and outlining climate change pressures on those values. Deliverables or major milestones include a values matrix and a current condition report.

Plan Development

Plan development for FLP includes determining objectives, indicators, and targets for selected values. Key activities include modelling desired future forest condition scenarios, identifying climate change implications and mitigation strategies, and building agreement on a preferred future forest scenario. Preparing a draft plan that includes desired outcomes, planning guidelines, a monitoring plan and an evaluation of socio-economic implications is a core activity. This is supported by a public review and comment period, as well as the development of a First Nations engagement summary/shared story. A Final Draft FLP and Recommendations document is the core deliverable of this phase.

Plan Establishment

Plan establishment for FLP includes the review and evaluation of the Final Draft FLP, leading to a plan establishment decision by the Chief Forester. An FLP Order establishing the FLP is the core deliverable for this stage.

Implementation and Monitoring

Implementation and monitoring of an FLP includes the development and approval of a Forest Operation Plan(s) and the implementation of an FLP monitoring strategy. A five-year Public Report is the core deliverable for this stage.
