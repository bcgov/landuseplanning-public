Water Planning

The B.C. government is committed to working in partnership with Indigenous governments and in collaboration with communities, local governments, industry, and other stakeholders to resolve watershed-specific challenges. The Water Sustainability Act has several area-based tools that can support watershed planning, regional water management, and watershed governance.

Planning for water use can improve the health of people, plants and animals in B.C. and can influence our economy, energy production, recreation, and irrigation.

The Water Sustainability Act (WSA) significantly reformed water management when it came into effect in 2016. It introduced groundwater licensing and new area-based tools, such as Water Sustainability Plans and WSA Objectives, to support watershed planning, regional water management and watershed governance.

In addition to the WSA, other initiatives use water plans to protect aquatic ecosystems when developing communities and allocating water for use in agriculture, industry and energy production.

Working together, we can protect water sources, manage water demands, modernize water systems and infrastructure, and live “water smart”.

Water Objectives

WSA Objectives are enabled under WSA s.43 . They are area-based planning tools that can promote positive outcomes for water and communities across a range of land and water planning and decision processes. They can be established to sustain water quality and quantity for specified uses of water and to sustain aquatic ecosystems. WSA Objectives would be considered by decision-makers for a variety of decisions and statutes.

Water Sustainability Plans

Water Sustainability Plans are enabled under WSA s.64-85 . They are initiated by ministerial order and can provide for extensive changes in how a watershed is managed. A planning area can include both public and private land.

Water Sustainability Plans can be developed to address conflicts between water users, the needs of users and environmental flow needs, risks to water quality or to aquatic ecosystem health, or identify restoration measures for a damaged aquatic ecosystem. Both WSA Objectives and Water Sustainability Plans enable a “whole of watershed” management approach.

Water Sustainability Plans can include regulations that apply to all of part of the water plan area. These plan regulations are described in WSA ss. 76-85 .
